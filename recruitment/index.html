Types of recruitment help

  • Contingency recruiters: you pay only when a candidate they introduced is hired.
  • Retained or executive search: you pay in stages for a dedicated search, often for senior roles.
  • Staffing and temp agencies: provide contract or temporary workers.
  • RPO: a provider runs some or all of your recruitment as an ongoing service.

Hiring in another country

Before you hire abroad, decide how you’ll legally engage the person: through your own local entity, an employer of record, or as an independent contractor where that genuinely fits. Pay, notice periods, benefits and right-to-work checks all follow local rules. Your recruiter should know the local market.

Questions to ask a recruiter

  • Do you specialise in this type of role and location?
  • What are your fee terms, and is there a rebate or replacement if the hire leaves early?
  • How do you assess candidates, and how do you support diverse shortlists?
  • Are you a member of a trade body, such as the REC in the UK or the American Staffing Association in the US?

What affects the cost

Fees are usually a percentage of first-year salary for permanent hires, a margin on top of pay for contract staff, or a monthly fee for RPO. Seniority, scarcity of skills and exclusivity all affect the rate. Get the terms of business in writing before you see CVs.

Frequently asked questions

What does a recruitment agency charge?
It depends on the model and the role. Ask each agency for its written terms, including rebates if a hire leaves early.
Should I use more than one agency?
For hard-to-fill roles, an exclusive or retained arrangement often gets more commitment. For volume hiring, several agencies can widen the pool.
Can a recruiter help with hiring abroad?
Yes, if they know that market. Pair them with an HR or payroll provider, or an employer of record, so the hire is set up correctly.
