Types of funding help

  • Finding and writing grant applications.
  • Preparing loan applications and finding lenders.
  • Investor readiness: business plans, financial models and data rooms.
  • Pitch decks and pitch coaching.
  • Introductions to angels, funds or crowdfunding platforms.

Official funding starting points

  • UK: Innovate UK and the British Business Bank.
  • US: the Small Business Administration (SBA) and SBIR/STTR research programmes.
  • EU: Horizon Europe and the European Innovation Council, plus national and regional schemes.

Check how advisers are regulated and paid

  • In many countries, arranging investments or giving investment advice is regulated: for example by the FCA in the UK, and by the SEC and FINRA (broker-dealer rules) in the US. Ask how the adviser is authorised for the work they’ll do.
  • Understand the fee: fixed fee, retainer or success fee. Be cautious of large upfront fees with no clear deliverables.
  • Make sure grant writers know the specific programme’s rules and deadlines.

What affects the cost

The type and size of funding, how much preparation your business needs, and the fee model. Grant writing and investor-readiness work are often fixed fees; brokers and placement agents may charge success fees where permitted.

Frequently asked questions

Can an adviser guarantee funding?
No. Funders make their own decisions. A good adviser improves your chances by matching you to the right funding and strengthening your application.
Do I need a pitch deck and financial model?
For equity investment, almost always. Lenders and grant bodies usually ask for a business plan and forecasts too.
What is a success fee?
A fee paid only if you secure funding, usually a percentage. Whether it’s allowed depends on the type of funding and local rules.
