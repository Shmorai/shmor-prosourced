Tasks you can hand to a virtual assistant

  • Inbox, calendar and travel management.
  • Customer service by email, chat or phone.
  • Data entry, CRM updates and reporting.
  • Research, presentations and document formatting.
  • Social media scheduling and basic design.
  • Supporting your bookkeeper with receipts and invoices.

Set them up safely

  • Use a password manager and give access only to what they need.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication for every shared account.
  • Sign a confidentiality agreement and, where personal data is involved, a data processing agreement.
  • Write down your processes so work stays consistent.

Hiring someone abroad directly? Check whether they are a contractor or an employee under local rules. Our guide to contractor or employee explains the tests.

Freelance VA, agency or dedicated team?

  • Freelance VA: direct relationship, flexible hours.
  • VA agency: vetted assistants, cover for holidays and a replacement if it isn’t working.
  • Dedicated offshore or nearshore team: several people for larger, ongoing workloads, often managed by the provider.

What affects the cost

Skills and experience, hours, location and time-zone overlap, language requirements and whether you hire directly or through an agency. Assistants are usually paid hourly, or by a monthly package of hours.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours of help do I need?
List your recurring tasks and time them for a week or two. Many businesses start with a small monthly package and grow it as they hand over more.
Is a virtual assistant an employee?
It depends on how the work is arranged and the law where they live. Agencies usually employ or contract the assistant themselves, which simplifies this for you.
Can a virtual assistant work in my time zone?
Yes. Many work set hours to overlap with yours, or cover hours when you’re offline.
