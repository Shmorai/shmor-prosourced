What tax advisers help with

  • Corporate and business tax planning within the rules.
  • VAT and GST registrations, returns and cross-border rules.
  • US sales tax: where you have “nexus” and must collect it.
  • International structures, permanent establishment and double-tax treaties.
  • Transfer pricing between companies in the same group.
  • Tax investigations, enquiries and disputes.
  • Incentives such as research and development tax relief.

Key cross-border rules to know

  • EU VAT: the One Stop Shop (OSS) lets businesses selling to consumers in other EU countries report VAT through one registration. Imports up to €150 can use the Import One Stop Shop (IOSS).
  • US sales tax: since the Supreme Court’s 2018 Wayfair decision, states can require remote sellers to collect sales tax once they pass economic thresholds, which vary by state.
  • Transfer pricing: most countries follow the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, based on the arm’s-length principle.
  • Global minimum tax: the OECD Pillar Two rules target large groups with annual revenue of €750 million or more.

Credentials to look for

  • UK: Chartered Tax Advisers (CTA) and members of the CIOT or ATT.
  • US: CPAs, Enrolled Agents and tax attorneys can represent taxpayers before the IRS.
  • Germany: Steuerberater; other countries have their own protected titles.
  • Everywhere: ask about experience with your specific countries and transaction types.

What affects the cost

Complexity, the number of countries, deadlines and whether it’s a one-off question or ongoing compliance. Advisory work is often hourly or a fixed project fee; ongoing filings are often monthly or annual packages.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a tax adviser or an accountant?
An accountant handles everyday accounts and returns. A tax adviser goes deeper on specific or cross-border tax questions. Many firms offer both.
When must I register for VAT in another EU country?
It depends on what you sell, to whom and how. The OSS covers many distance sales to consumers. Take advice for your situation.
Is tax planning legal?
Using reliefs and structures as the law intends is legal. Aggressive schemes that rely on artificial arrangements can be challenged. Reputable advisers will tell you the difference.
