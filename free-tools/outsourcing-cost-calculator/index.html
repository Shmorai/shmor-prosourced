What to include in the in-house cost

  • Employer on-costs: payroll taxes or National Insurance, social security, pension or 401(k) contributions, health insurance and other benefits. These vary by country, so use your own figures or ask your accountant.
  • Overheads: laptop, software licences, office space, training and management time.
  • Recruitment: agency fees, job ads and interview time.
  • Hidden time: holidays, sickness and the time it takes a new hire to get fully up to speed.

When outsourcing tends to make sense

  • The work is specialist, occasional or seasonal.
  • You need it in a country where you don’t have a company.
  • You want to scale up or down quickly.
  • You need cover across several skills that one hire couldn’t provide.

When the work is core, constant and needs close day-to-day control, hiring often wins. Many businesses use a mix of both.

Frequently asked questions

Is the calculator accurate for my country?
It only uses the figures you enter, so it works in any country and currency. The accuracy depends on your inputs, especially employer on-costs, which differ by country.
Do you store what I type?
No. The calculation happens in your browser, and nothing is sent to us.
