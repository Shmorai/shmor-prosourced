Types of logistics partner

  • 3PL fulfilment: stores your stock, picks, packs and ships orders, and handles returns.
  • Freight forwarder: arranges sea, air, road or rail transport for larger shipments.
  • Customs broker: prepares customs declarations and helps calculate duties and taxes.
  • Couriers and parcel aggregators: discounted rates for parcels.

Cross-border basics

  • Incoterms® 2020, published by the International Chamber of Commerce, set out who pays and takes risk at each stage of a shipment. Agree them in every contract.
  • EORI numbers are needed to move goods into or out of the UK and EU.
  • US: customs brokers are licensed by U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP).
  • Product codes: the right tariff (HS) code affects duty and paperwork.

Questions to ask a 3PL

  • Which platforms and marketplaces do you integrate with?
  • What are your cut-off times and order accuracy measures?
  • How do you handle returns and damaged stock?
  • Where are your warehouses, and can you serve other countries?
  • How are storage, pick and pack, and packaging charged?

What affects the cost

Storage space, order volume, items per order, packaging, carriers, destinations, duties and value-added services. Ask for a full rate card and model it against a typical month.

Frequently asked questions

When should I use a 3PL?
When packing orders yourself takes too much time or space, when you need faster delivery, or when you want to store stock closer to customers in other countries.
Do I need a customs broker?
Not always, but they help with complex or high-value imports and keep declarations accurate.
What are Incoterms?
Standard trade terms from the International Chamber of Commerce that define who handles transport, insurance, duties and risk.
