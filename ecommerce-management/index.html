What ecommerce specialists manage

  • Store set-up, themes, apps and conversion improvements.
  • Marketplace accounts such as Amazon, eBay, Etsy and others.
  • Product listings, images and keyword optimisation.
  • Marketplace and shopping ads.
  • Inventory, pricing and promotions.
  • Customer reviews and seller-account health.

Selling into new countries

  • EU VAT: the One Stop Shop (OSS) and Import One Stop Shop (IOSS) simplify VAT on many sales to EU consumers.
  • EU product safety: the General Product Safety Regulation has applied since 13 December 2024 and requires an EU-based responsible person for many products sold online.
  • US sales tax: states can require remote sellers to collect sales tax once they pass economic thresholds.
  • Logistics: plan duties, returns and fulfilment. See logistics and fulfilment.

For tax questions, talk to a tax adviser.

Questions to ask

  • Which platforms and countries do you manage today?
  • Will we keep ownership of all accounts, ad accounts and data?
  • How do you report on sales, ad spend and profit?
  • How do you handle account-health issues and policy changes?

What affects the cost

Number of products, platforms and countries, ad spend, content creation and whether operations are included. Agencies charge monthly fees, a percentage of sales or ad spend, or a mix.

Frequently asked questions

Should I sell on Amazon or my own store?
Many brands do both: marketplaces for reach, their own store for margin and customer relationships.
What is an EU responsible person?
Under EU product safety rules, many products sold to EU consumers need an economic operator established in the EU who is responsible for compliance.
How are ecommerce agencies paid?
Usually a monthly fee, a percentage of sales or ad spend, or a combination. Compare total cost against expected profit.
